Research

AI runs on a physical backbone. I trace it end to end: from the data center, to the power plants and water that feed it, to the air people breathe and the communities that carry the costs.

01

Energy & emissions

How much electricity AI data centers use, which power plants supply it, and the carbon that follows.

Key work: Nature Sustainability, 2026 · 100+ citations

02

Water

The two water footprints of data centers: cooling on site, and the water used to generate their power.

Key work: Hidden Water Geography, 2026

03

Air pollution & health

Where fine-particle pollution from the plants powering AI and crypto travels, and who breathes it.

Key work: Nature Communications, 2025

04

Policy: who pays

Turning evidence into decisions on siting and power sourcing, and sharing the costs fairly.

Key work: American Journal of Public Health, 2026

Real-world impact Our health assessment of a proposed 3,500-MW gas plant in rural Virginia estimated pollution reaching over 1.2 million people. After the findings went public, the project was withdrawn. Read the story in Grist

About

I’m a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health, working in Francesca Dominici’s research group. I study how artificial intelligence and digital infrastructure interact with energy systems, water resources, the environment, and public health, with a focus on quantifying impacts, identifying trade-offs, and informing evidence-based policy.

My toolkit combines machine learning, statistical modeling, complex networks, and air-quality modeling with large-scale environmental and public-health data. I work across public health, computer science, environmental science, and energy systems.
